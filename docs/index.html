Physics-Informed Intent-Aware Human-Robot Collaborative Transportation With Forward-Looking Adaptation

Abstract

Accurate human-intent following is a fundamental capability for quadruped mobile manipulators in collaborative payload transportation. Existing methods typically infer human intent using direct force sensing or accurate dynamic models, and compensate for disturbances reactively after their effects appear in the robot state. However, two key challenges remain underexplored. First, human intent must be distinguished from non-intent forces, such as external disturbances, using only proprioceptive observations while maintaining consistency with whole-body dynamics. Second, disturbances should be compensated for before they cause deviations from the intended collaborative motion. To address these, we propose Physics-Informed and Forward-looking Adaptation (PIFA), an approach for collaborative transportation. PIFA encodes proprioceptive histories to capture temporal motion and interaction context and jointly predicts human motion intent, interaction wrench, non-intent forces, and the future robot state. These predictions are constrained by the robot's whole-body dynamics to promote physical consistency. PIFA further converts the predicted intent into coordinated leg and arm commands while using the predicted non-intent forces and future state to compensate for disturbances. Extensive experiments in simulation and on physical robots demonstrate that PIFA enables more accurate human-intent tracking and force estimation while substantially reducing end-effector interaction forces under payload variations and external disturbances compared with existing methods.
